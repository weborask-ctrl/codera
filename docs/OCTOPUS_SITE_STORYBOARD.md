# Codera — octopus site storyboard

Superseded direction: Marcus rejected the disappear/reappear framing below.
Use `OCTOPUS_DIVE_IMPLEMENTATION.md` for the current continuous-descent plan.
The six-act text below is retained only as the earlier proposal.

Working draft, 2026-09-20. This document defines the page journey before the
production octopus is sculpted, rigged or animated. It is not an approved final
design. The approved ocean remains the visual baseline.

## Direction

The site is one continuous dive with one recognizable octopus. The animal is a
character and spatial guide, not a logo or a decoration placed beside every
heading. Its position, gaze and distance change the scale of the world. Long
reading passages deliberately reduce motion.

The page keeps the truthful Codera content already present in the repository:
five interactive studio demos, the actual service scope and prices, WordPress
work, the short process, Marcus and Peter, and the project enquiry. The former
city metaphor is not carried into this direction.

## Six acts

### 1. Hero — first contact

**Content:** Codera, the main promise, one short supporting sentence and the
primary action. The current idea “Vaša firma je lepšia, než ukazuje váš web” is
the copy baseline, not yet the locked line.

**Picture:** A very wide underwater frame. The octopus starts deeper in the
water, approaches enough to reveal both eyes and settles into a calm frontal
pose. The title occupies clear negative space; the animal never passes behind
the letterforms.

**Scroll:** Slow camera approach, small mantle breathing, delayed arm response.
The first movement establishes scale rather than showing every capability.

### 2. Work — five demonstrations

**Content:** Observatórium, Kancelária, Pražiareň, Štúdio and WordPress. Each
item shows its real image, name, concise description and link into the working
demo.

**Picture:** At the entrance, the camera passes one arm and arrives in a calmer,
wider volume of water. The octopus retreats out of the reading plane. A distant
silhouette or an occasional slow arm may preserve continuity, but it must not
compete with the projects.

**Scroll:** Projects scroll normally. There is no animated transition between
individual examples and no forced horizontal scroll. The underwater world may
continue its low-frequency light and particles. At the end, one arm crossing
the far edge initiates the next passage.

### 3. Studio — who is behind the work

**Content:** Codera is Marcus and Peter; design and development are handled as
one process. Keep this concise and factual. No invented roles, years, awards or
client claims.

**Picture:** A controlled side close-up of the head and one eye. This is the
first shot that requires convincing eyelids, skin and corneal response. The
text sits in a large quiet field on the opposite side.

**Scroll:** The camera moves beside the animal rather than rotating the animal
like a product viewer. One arm moves behind the copy plane to establish depth.

### 4. Services — what Codera builds

**Content:** Landing pages, company sites, ecommerce, portfolios and redesigns;
the current three price levels and WordPress work can open progressively inside
this act. The visitor must be able to compare the offer without chasing moving
cards.

**Picture:** The camera pulls wider. The octopus moves diagonally through the
background while three large content compositions occupy the foreground.
Colour from the skin and water separates the planes without glass-panel clutter.

**Scroll:** One continuous travel into the act, followed by stable reading. The
content itself does not orbit or float. Motion resumes only after the last offer
has been read.

### 5. Process — from brief to launch

**Content:** consultation, first direction, build and launch. The present
promises (response within 24 hours, first proposal within 72 hours, typical
delivery wording) remain linked to the shared site configuration.

**Picture:** A higher camera angle reveals the arm layout as one coherent
organism. Three or four large statements appear along a gentle vertical route.
No numbered micro-labels; each real heading carries the hierarchy.

**Scroll:** The camera rises while the octopus descends, producing separation
without a hard cut. Arm motion is restrained and follows the body with delay.

### 6. Contact — return and release

**Content:** A memorable closing statement, the project action, email and
telephone. The form can open as a focused layer without destroying the scene.

**Picture:** The octopus returns as a complete silhouette at a greater distance,
then turns slightly toward the open water. Surface light becomes broader and
warmer. The final frame is spacious enough for the contact action.

**Scroll:** A final controlled pullback, not another aggressive zoom. Ambient
motion may continue after the visitor stops. Reduced-motion users receive the
same final compositions as static frames.

## Motion contract

- The octopus appears in every major passage, but is visually dominant only in
  the hero, studio close-up and final return.
- Section transitions may move camera and animal. Reading states settle.
- The five work examples use ordinary vertical scroll with no individual
  transition animation.
- Text remains DOM content. It is never baked into video or texture assets.
- Scroll motion is reversible and directly mapped to progress; idle breathing
  remains time-based and subtle.
- Large headings remain readable and prominent. Final pages contain no small
  decorative eyebrows or numbered section captions.

## What this requires from the production model

1. Recognizable frontal, three-quarter and side silhouettes.
2. Both eyes built as separate layered parts with deformable surrounding tissue.
3. A mantle able to breathe without collapsing the head.
4. Eight independently controllable arm chains with stable volume and attached
   sucker rows; hero-facing arms need the highest detail.
5. Three performance tiers: close-up head, full desktop animal and simplified
   mobile/static fallback.
6. Authored poses for hero settle, portfolio exit, side close-up, diagonal
   background swim, top-view descent and final turn.

## First prototype gate

Before further Blender work, build these acts with the current animal as a
proxy and validate three passages: hero to work, work to studio, and process to
contact. Approve composition, safe text areas and scroll pace first. Only then
lock the production sculpt, rig and texture scope.
