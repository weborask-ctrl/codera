# Hospitality / Sensory

**Archetype:** atmosphere first — photography, warmth, appetite, place.
Studied exemplars: BelArosa Chalet ("dusk on alpine vellum"), Lamanna
Bakery ("neon circus tent on a Naples sidewalk"), Essie Wine ("dusty
rose chapbook"), Hartzler Dairy, Aspelin Reitan, AATHER.

**Emotional tone:** anticipation of a sensory experience — taste, rest,
view, welcome. The page must make you *feel the place* before you book
or order.

**Typical audience / business fit:** hotels and chalets, restaurants and
bakeries, wineries, travel experiences, spas, venues and weddings, food
brands with terroir. Conversion = booking/reservation, driven by desire
not by features.

**Poor-fit contexts:** utility software, data products, urgent services;
also fails when a venue has NO good photography — this genre cannot run
on stock or renders; commission the shoot or pick another family.

**Canvas / background behavior:** two temperature poles: refined —
parchment/linen + one deep atmospheric tone (BelArosa's teal, wine
tones); exuberant — white ground + loud appetizing color bands
(Lamanna's orange/yellow). Both alternate full-bleed mood bands with
calm content bands.

**Color logic:** palette FROM the place — alpine slate, honey, terracotta,
vine green, flour white; one deep tone as brand anchor; food contexts may
run hotter saturation (appetite likes warmth: orange/red/gold family);
color never fights the photography — it frames it.

**Typography logic:** serif display for refined hospitality
(Giovanni/Canela class — "a serif headline is a moment"); expressive
display sans for casual/food energy (Lamanna's grotesk-spatial-black);
uppercase tracked eyebrows (CUISINE, PHILOSOPHY) structure the
storytelling; body always a calm sans.

**Spacing / density:** slow luxury pacing for stays (64–80px+, one scene
per screen); busier rhythm acceptable for food/casual (menu boards ARE
density) — but even Lamanna separates loud bands with calm ones.

**Layout / composition:** full-bleed hero with place photography or deep
color + centered serif statement; 50/50 splits (photo bleeding to edge /
text block); circular/arch masks as jewelry; menu/offer tables set like
printed cards; booking CTA persistent but polite.

**Imagery direction:** THE core asset — golden-hour place photography,
food close-ups with steam and texture, hands and craft, staff warmth;
color-graded to one mood; video loops of place ambience welcome; drone
establishing shots used once, not everywhere.

**Component language:** ghost/outlined pill CTAs for refined (BOOK),
chunky filled buttons for casual; date/guest pickers styled to the brand
(the booking widget is where most hospitality brands break — style it);
menus as typographic set pieces, not PDFs.

**Motion behavior:** slow crossfades and Ken-Burns drift on photography;
parallax gentle; refined = settle, casual = a little bounce; ambient
video muted and subtle. Motion evokes the place's tempo — chalet ≠
bakery.

**Mobile adaptation:** booking flows one-thumb: sticky BOOK/RESERVE bar,
tap-to-call prominent, maps one tap; photography art-directed for
portrait crops (compose for 4:5 at shoot time); menus as native pages,
never zoomed PDFs.

**Strengths:** highest emotional conversion of any family; photography
does the selling; price elasticity (great atmosphere justifies rates);
naturally differentiated (every place is unique).

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** stock photography (instantly fatal);
PDF menus; booking widget in raw third-party skin; gloom-dark "luxury"
theme killing appetite/warmth; density panic listing every amenity above
the fold; music/autoplay with sound; slideshow heroes cycling too fast to
feel calm.

**Combination guidance:** luxury grammar for premium stays (native
sibling); warm-editorial for wineries/food storytelling; organic-natural
for farm/terroir; high-color-expressive for casual food energy
(Lamanna); image-led is essentially this family minus the booking layer.
Avoid clinical, corporate, and dark-cinematic (except one dusk-mood band
used deliberately).
