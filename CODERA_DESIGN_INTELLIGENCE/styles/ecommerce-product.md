# Ecommerce / Product-first

**Archetype:** the product is the hero; the UI recedes to a mount.
Studied exemplars: Peak Design ("gallery wall, half lit"), Apple
(watch page), Cowboy, Acme Cups ("editorial ceramics gallery"),
A-dam, Break Maiden's tiles, Impossible Foods.

**Emotional tone:** desire, clarity, confidence in the object. The page
behaves like a well-lit shop shelf: you look at things, not at chrome.

**Typical audience / business fit:** D2C brands, hardware, furniture,
fashion basics, food/beverage DTC, any catalogue where photography can
carry the sell. Conversion-focused but taste-led.

**Poor-fit contexts:** service businesses without product imagery,
content/media brands, products that photograph poorly (software sold as
boxes), commodity catalogs competing purely on price/density (marketplace
grammar differs).

**Canvas / background behavior:** white or near-white shelf; near-black
acts for drama (Peak Design's split white/black panels); flat color mats
matched to SKU flavor (Finn, fal.ai) — the mat system gives each product
its own room without leaving the brand.

**Color logic:** UI monochrome; PRODUCT carries color. One accent for
commerce signals only (sale/urgency), used with scarcity ("full-color
Ember Red on more than one element per viewport never"). Category color
coding via mats/labels, not via UI chrome.

**Typography logic:** grotesque body + optional display voice (condensed
uppercase eyebrow labels are the genre's native accent — bryant-class
compressed caps with positive tracking); product names weight 600–700;
prices honest and readable, tabular figures; serif display only when the
brand is editorial-luxury.

**Spacing / density:** dual-mode: editorial sections breathe (80px+),
product grids are tight and even (4-col, 24–32px gaps). The mode switch
is explicit — never blend the two rhythms in one band.

**Layout / composition:** 50/50 split hero (statement + product); uniform
product-card grids (equal crops! mixed crops read chaotic); filter bars
as pill chips; PDP = gallery left / sticky buy-box right; editorial
interludes between commerce blocks to pace the scroll.

**Imagery direction:** the discipline that makes the genre — consistent
studio photography (same light, same backdrop family, same crop logic);
texture close-ups for material trust; lifestyle full-bleeds as separate
acts; no radius inside hero imagery, 4–8px in grids; NEVER mixed
photography grammars in one grid.

**Component language:** flat borderless product cards separated by
whitespace ("separate with 24px+ whitespace, not with containers");
pills for filters/badges; buy CTA is the one loud element on a PDP;
size/variant selectors big and honest; trust strip (shipping, returns)
quiet but present.

**Motion behavior:** utilitarian polish — image crossfades on hover
(second angle), cart drawer slide, subtle grid stagger on load; zero
theatrics between the user and the buy button; motion never delays
add-to-cart feedback.

**Mobile adaptation:** where the money is — 2-col grids, swipeable PDP
galleries, sticky add-to-cart bar, filters in a bottom sheet; image
weight budgeted hard (srcset mandatory); thumb-zone CTA placement.

**Strengths:** photography does the persuasion; grids scale to any
catalogue size; conversion patterns are well-proven; editorial interludes
lift perceived brand far above marketplace competitors.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** inconsistent product photography
(the #1 killer); UI accent colors competing with product colors; card
chrome (borders+shadows+badges) drowning the object; density panic on
the homepage; sale-badge pollution; PDPs where chrome outweighs imagery.

**Combination guidance:** minimal-swiss chassis is native (Cowboy);
luxury grammar for premium lines (ghost CTAs, serif moments, slower
rhythm); dark-cinematic act for flagship staging (Apple pattern:
dark hero acts → white detail bands); playful mats for kids/food SKUs.
The genre absorbs others' surfaces gladly BUT the product-photography
discipline is non-negotiable in every hybrid.
