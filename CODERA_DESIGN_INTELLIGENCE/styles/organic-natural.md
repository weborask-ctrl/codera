# Organic / Natural

**Archetype:** earth palettes, botanical imagery, unhurried rhythm —
nature as both subject and system. Studied exemplars: Felt ("topographic
atlas at dusk"), Oevra ("sage greenhouse at dawn"), Arcadia ("forest
observatory"), A-WARE, ddna, Evervault's opposite pole.

**Emotional tone:** groundedness, honesty, sustainability, patience.
The design breathes like a landscape — nothing synthetic, nothing rushed.

**Typical audience / business fit:** sustainability and climate brands,
agriculture/food origin, outdoor gear, botanical skincare, eco-building,
land/mapping products, wellness with plant positioning, NGOs with nature
missions.

**Poor-fit contexts:** high-tech precision products (unless deliberately
contrasted), urgency commerce, financial gravity, nightlife; greenwash
risk — this grammar on a non-sustainable business reads as deception and
audiences increasingly punish it.

**Canvas / background behavior:** two poles: sunlit — warm paper/bone
(#f1efdf, cream) with sage/moss accents; dusk — deep green canvas as the
brand world (Felt's #314218 moss with surfaces stepping Moss → Fern →
Lichen — elevation through the green scale itself, no shadows).

**Color logic:** palettes sampled from actual terrain — moss, sage,
clay, bark, wheat, sky; ONE warm accent as the compass needle (Felt's
amber #dc8c46 "exclusively for filled CTAs — never a surface fill");
desaturated rather than candy; near-black avoided in favor of deep
greens/browns as ink.

**Typography logic:** serif display gives the field-journal voice
(GT Alpina thin at 86px/0.88 lh — "carved" compression); grotesque
utility body with slight positive tracking for dark-canvas legibility;
alternatively humanist sans throughout for product-led eco brands.
No techno/condensed faces.

**Spacing / density:** magazine-calm: 80px sections, centered measures,
one landscape per screen; data (maps, metrics) presented in framed light
panels against the canvas rather than dense dashboards.

**Layout / composition:** centered atlas-style stacks; full-width
"fold-out" reveals for product/maps; contour-line and topographic motifs
as the decorative system (not literal leaf clipart); photography bands
alternating with typographic rest.

**Imagery direction:** real landscape/botanical photography with honest
color; macro texture (soil, grain, leaf); product-in-environment; maps
and satellite imagery as legitimate heroes (Felt); illustration only in
scientific-etching or line-drawing styles. No neon, no sterile studio
seamless.

**Component language:** modest radii (6px cards, 20px pill CTAs);
hairlines and surface-stepping over shadows; buttons in the accent used
sparingly; badges as stamped/etched labels; light product panels framed
against deep canvases.

**Motion behavior:** wind-tempo — slow drifts, soft parallax on
landscape, gentle grow/reveal on data; nothing snappy or mechanical;
scroll advances like walking a trail, with rests.

**Mobile adaptation:** photography art-directed for portrait; deep-canvas
mode needs contrast care outdoors (sunlight readability — test at max
brightness); maps/data panels become full-screen takeovers rather than
embedded miniatures.

**Strengths:** instant values-signal; warm differentiation from tech
sameness; photography+palette carry most of the identity; pairs
naturally with storytelling content.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** greenwash grammar on hollow claims;
leaf-clipart literalism; kraft-paper texture JPEGs; palette drifting
into murk (all-desaturated = lifeless — the amber needle exists for a
reason); dark-green canvas with low-contrast text; stock nature panoramas
everyone has seen.

**Combination guidance:** warm-editorial is the native voice (field
journal); hospitality for agritourism/wineries; healthcare for botanical
wellness; minimal-swiss lends rigor to climate-tech (Arcadia). With
tech-saas, keep the green world as marketing shell and let the product
keep its own clean UI (Felt does exactly this split).
