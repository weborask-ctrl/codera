# Brutalist / Experimental

**Archetype:** oversized type, raw edges, rules broken on purpose — the
style that proves intent by violating convention precisely. Studied
exemplars: Break Maiden ("oversized white type carved into a black
gallery wall"), TWOMUCH.STUDIO, The1 Amsterdam, Martin Laxenaire,
Danilo Rodrigues, 12th Berlin Biennale.

**Emotional tone:** defiance, confidence, art-world credibility, energy.
It says "we don't need to look safe" — which only works when execution
is flawless.

**Typical audience / business fit:** creative studios and agencies,
cultural institutions and festivals, fashion-forward brands, portfolios,
music/art projects, brands courting design-literate youth. The audience
must ENJOY being challenged.

**Poor-fit contexts:** trust-critical services (health, finance, legal),
older or task-focused audiences, complex products needing guidance,
conversion funnels — brutalism taxes usability and the tax must be
affordable.

**Canvas / background behavior:** absolute grounds — pure #000 or pure
#fff, sometimes one violent color field (Biennale's iris violet);
no gentle off-whites, no gradients; sections separated by scale shifts
and hard cuts, not by tinted bands.

**Color logic:** monochrome + at most one loud interruption (lime disc,
blood red slide); photography/art supplies all other color; NO accent
system, no semantic palette on the marketing surface — restraint at the
palette level funds the violence at the type level.

**Typography logic:** the genre IS typography: display faces at
96–153px+ (condensed grotesques — Martin/Anton/Druk class, or Monument
Extended wide), single weight, lh ~1.0, bleeding to viewport edges;
body in a quiet neo-grotesque at 16–22px single weight; inline glyphs/
symbols AS type (arrows, sunbursts, parenthetical icons at display
scale).

**Spacing / density:** violent contrast — massive negative space around
the statement, then tight dense clusters (uneven grids); uniform
comfortable spacing is the enemy; asymmetry mandatory but ALIGNED (broken
grids still have a grid underneath — that's the craft).

**Layout / composition:** asymmetric two-zone heroes (colossal headline
left, whisper paragraph right); irregular magazine-cut grids ("columns
deliberately uneven"); elements crossing edges; marquee/ticker text;
footers as giant wordmarks. Every rule-break must look chosen, not
broken.

**Imagery direction:** art-grade photography or renders presented raw —
edge-to-edge, 0px radius, no frames; scale jumps (tiny caption under
huge image); glitch/scan/print artifacts as texture when earned; no
polite rounded product cards.

**Component language:** zero radius everywhere; no filled buttons —
typographic ghost links with hover inversions/underlines; hairlines as
the only chrome; cursor experiments and hover reveals native here;
forms minimal (this genre rarely sells directly).

**Motion behavior:** the experimental license lives here — kinetic type,
liquid/WebGL distortion, aggressive marquees, snap transitions; BUT the
static-frame rule still applies: pause any moment and the composition
must hold. Motion earns the "experimental" label; layout earns the
respect.

**Mobile adaptation:** the genre's hardest test — 153px headlines clamp
to ~40–56px but MUST keep edge-bleed and asymmetry (centered-shrunken
brutalism = dead); irregular grids become alternating offsets; hover-
dependent reveals need tap equivalents; keep one violent moment per
screen.

**Strengths:** unmatched memorability; instant creative credibility;
cheap in assets (type does everything); portfolio sites in this genre
get shared.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** brutalism as sloppiness (bad
kerning, accidental overflow ≠ style); usability collapse (unfindable
nav as a "statement"); trend-copying Monument-Extended-on-black without
a reason; violence everywhere so nothing lands; accessibility ignored
(this genre CAN pass contrast — pure black/white is AAA).

**Combination guidance:** with light-editorial → the fashionable "warm
brutalism" (huge type on cream, softer ink); with industrial → poster-
architecture (The1); with image-led → gallery violence (Break Maiden);
with dark-cinematic → shared austerity. Never dilute into corporate,
clinical, or pastel contexts — a half-brutalist trust page satisfies
no one.
