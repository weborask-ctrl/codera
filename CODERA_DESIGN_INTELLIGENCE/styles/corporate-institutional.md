# Corporate / Institutional

**Archetype:** legible authority — system over expression, trust through
order. Studied exemplars: Atlassian ("confetti-edged engineering
blueprint"), NEVERHACK, Arcadia, CQCM, Workable, SAP Good Energy,
Galileo-ft.

**Emotional tone:** competence, stability, scale, procedural trust. The
design says "we will still exist in ten years and your data is safe."

**Typical audience / business fit:** enterprise software, banking and
insurance, legal, government and civic bodies, large consultancies,
energy/logistics, B2B with procurement committees. Multiple stakeholders
must all find it acceptable — the design optimizes for zero objections
plus one memorable signature.

**Poor-fit contexts:** lifestyle brands, creative studios, anything
selling emotion or taste; small startups trying to look big usually
produce generic-corporate instead (the most common bad outcome in the
whole catalogue).

**Canvas / background behavior:** white/light-grey base with structured
alternating bands (white → fog → tinted band); dark navy panels as
controlled emphasis moments, framed and bounded — never a whole-site
theme.

**Color logic:** ONE brand action color on every button/link/focus
(Atlassian #1868db discipline); a small set of decorative accents that
are FRAMED as decoration (confetti edges, section dividers) and banned
from functional UI; semantic states standardized (success/warn/error);
navy-tinted shadows instead of black when elevation exists.

**Typography logic:** paired custom or licensed sans family (Text +
Display cuts); body 16px/1.5; display 40–80px weight 400–500 (heavy
black display weights read dated-corporate); no serifs unless heritage
institution (then serif display + sans UI). Generous, systematic scale
with documented steps.

**Spacing / density:** medium density, high consistency: 64–80px
sections, 24px card padding, 1200px max-width, 8pt grid. The system must
survive hundreds of pages and dozens of authors — predictability beats
expressiveness.

**Layout / composition:** repeatable section patterns (hero, logo strip,
3-col features, split feature, testimonial band, CTA band); 20px-radius
cards; product screenshots tilted or framed on solid brand-color mats;
mega-nav done soberly.

**Imagery direction:** real product UI, diagrammatic illustration in a
fixed geometric palette, photography of actual people at work (not
handshake stock); decorative geometry (Atlassian's torn confetti frames)
as the ONE expressive device.

**Component language:** complete, documented set — buttons (filled/
outline/ghost), inputs with visible labels and states, tabs, tables,
banners; pills for tags; 8–28px radii; accessibility-complete states
(focus rings, disabled, error). The component library IS the brand here.

**Motion behavior:** functional only — 150–250ms fades/slides, skeletons,
progress; one hero animation allowed; anything decorative-in-motion
raises procurement eyebrows. Reduced-motion parity mandatory.

**Mobile adaptation:** collapse to a boring, perfect single column; nav
into a disciplined drawer; tables to stacked cards; touch targets 44px+;
performance budget strict (these audiences use old corporate hardware).

**Strengths:** scales across teams and years; passes committees;
accessibility-friendly by construction; forgettable in the good sense —
nothing to object to; the single expressive device makes it memorable.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** generic-corporate (no signature
device at all); accent inflation (every department gets a color);
dark-navy panels metastasizing into a gloomy theme; stocky handshake
photography; radius/spacing drift across pages; hero jargon replacing
message hierarchy.

**Combination guidance:** absorbs ONE expressive subsystem to become
memorable: confetti geometry (Atlassian), editorial serif moments
(institutional-heritage), warm canvas (civic warmth — CQCM). With
tech-saas it merges seamlessly (shared component DNA). Never absorb
brutalist or playful wholesale — one bounded device only.
