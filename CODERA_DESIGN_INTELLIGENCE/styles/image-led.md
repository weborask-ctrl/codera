# Image-led / Art-directed

**Archetype:** photography carries everything; the UI goes silent.
Studied exemplars: Locomotive ("monochrome zine between blood-red
slides"), Simone Sniekers ("the image does all the work, the UI almost
disappears"), Break Maiden, BelArosa's photo bands, Wallpaper Projects,
Artandcommerce.

**Emotional tone:** immersion, atmosphere, editorial worship of the
image. The site behaves like a monograph or exhibition — you are
looking AT something, not using something.

**Typical audience / business fit:** photographers and image-makers,
fashion houses, film/production studios, art institutions, travel
storytelling, architecture portfolios, premium print brands — anywhere
the imagery IS the product or the proof.

**Poor-fit contexts:** businesses without commissioned-grade imagery
(fatal — this family cannot run on stock); information-dense products;
accessibility-critical services (text-scarce surfaces need extra care);
conversion-heavy funnels.

**Canvas / background behavior:** neutral silence — pure white gallery
or pure black theater; the canvas exists only to mount images; canvas
switches (white→black) mark exhibition rooms; nothing tinted, nothing
textured.

**Color logic:** UI strictly monochrome (Locomotive: "only photography
is allowed to scream — 0% colorfulness is a deliberate, ownable
constraint"); images supply ALL chroma and mood; if a brand color
exists it appears in one place (a logo, a hover) and nowhere else.

**Typography logic:** quiet captions vs display statements — nothing in
between: 12–16px metadata labels (often floating at image corners,
museum-catalogue style) and occasional 70–110px display moments; single
weight; zero tracking games; type must never compete with the image
tonally.

**Spacing / density:** images get room — full-bleed 100vh heroes OR
centered ~40% width objects in white oceans; 150px section padding;
sparse text blocks; the page alternates immersion (full-bleed) and
contemplation (small image, huge margin).

**Layout / composition:** 100vh image heroes with floating nav; uneven
editorial grids (2-col pairs, offset thirds); horizontal scroll
galleries where genuinely useful (with visible affordance); captions at
plan-positions around images; the composition logic is a printed
portfolio, not a website template.

**Imagery direction:** everything — and therefore ruthless curation:
one grade, one grammar, sequenced deliberately (wide → detail → human
rhythm); aspect ratios respected (no lazy center-crops); videos as
silent loops; EVERY image commissioned-grade or the family collapses.

**Component language:** near-none — text links, hairline rules, minimal
nav that floats over imagery (with scrim discipline for contrast);
0px radii; no cards (the image is the card); filters/indices as
typographic lists.

**Motion behavior:** image-serving only — slow reveals/unmasking on
scroll, subtle Ken-Burns drift, crossfade galleries, hover showing a
second frame; transitions BETWEEN images (shared-element continuity)
are the genre's signature craft; motion never decorates chrome.

**Mobile adaptation:** art-direct per breakpoint (compose portrait
crops at shoot time — auto-cropping landscape masterpieces is
malpractice); full-bleed preserved; hover-reveals become tap/scroll
reveals; nav-over-image needs contrast scrims checked on real screens.

**Strengths:** maximal atmosphere per byte of design; the work speaks
for itself; timeless (a good monograph never dates); minimal component
maintenance.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** weak images exposed by silence
(the family amplifies quality in BOTH directions); text-over-image
contrast failures; grids mixing image grammars; slideshow autoplay
rushing contemplation; missing alt/text hierarchy (screen readers meet
an empty page); UI chrome creeping in to "help" and killing the spell.

**Combination guidance:** essentially a MODE other families borrow:
light-editorial + images = the gallery standard; dark-cinematic +
images = the theater standard; hospitality/luxury use it as their
photo acts. As a standalone family it pairs with editorial typography
only — anything more is already too much.
