# Soft Pastel / Friendly

**Archetype:** low-saturation warmth — approachable, unthreatening,
breathable. Studied exemplars: 15five ("sunrise over a clean desk"),
Jasper's pastel mosaic, Finn's blush/sky washes, pampam.city ("old
explorer's notebook"), ElevenLabs' quiet end, Riverside's lavender.

**Emotional tone:** ease, kindness, optimism without noise. The design
equivalent of a soft-spoken competent person.

**Typical audience / business fit:** HR and people-ops software,
consumer wellness, family services, education, community products,
onboarding-heavy tools, brands for stressed users (the pastel promise:
"this will be easy").

**Poor-fit contexts:** power-user tools (reads underpowered), luxury
(pastel ≠ premium unless executed as warm-editorial), industrial/
technical authority, urgent/serious communication; male-skewing
performance categories often reject it (test, don't assume).

**Canvas / background behavior:** white or warm-white canvas with
pastel WASHES as section tints (blush, sky, mint, lavender at 10–20%
saturation feel); washes rotate per section like rooms; deep ink
sections rare and soft (midnight navy, not black).

**Color logic:** pastels are SURFACES, not text and not primary CTAs —
the action color is one deeper anchor (navy, violet, deep teal) that
gives the softness a spine; text ink warm and dark for contrast
(pastel-on-pastel text is the genre's death); 2–4 pastel families max,
mapped to categories/SKUs.

**Typography logic:** rounded or humanist sans (Figtree, Poppins-class,
Sofia, Larsseit); light-to-regular weights at display with tight
tracking to stay adult (72px at weight 300 = airy; at weight 700 =
corporate); body 16px+ generous; serif display only for the
storybook-editorial end.

**Spacing / density:** airy — 80px sections, roomy cards, few elements
per screen; density is the enemy of "this will be easy"; progressive
disclosure over information walls.

**Layout / composition:** centered hero stacks; card grids with pastel
mats; alternating wash bands; illustration/product screenshots in
rounded frames; wave/blob dividers used ONCE if at all (the 2019 blob
cliché is one blob from kitsch).

**Imagery direction:** soft-lit photography with pastel grading; flat
illustration with rounded geometry; product UI on pastel mats; avatars
and faces welcome (the genre is people-centric); nothing high-contrast
or dramatic.

**Component language:** pills and 8–16px radii; pastel chip tags with
dark ink text; primary CTA in the deep anchor color (flipping to pastel
on dark bands); soft 1px borders in warmed greys; sticker-shadows for
playful moments; forms big, kind, well-labeled.

**Motion behavior:** gentle floats and fades; soft spring on cards;
success states may bloom (subtle confetti/petals); nothing snappy or
mechanical; motion tempo relaxed but interactions still respond
instantly.

**Mobile adaptation:** natural fit (soft surfaces, big targets); watch
wash-band contrast in sunlight; keep the deep-anchor CTA persistent;
pastel category coding survives small screens better than text labels.

**Strengths:** lowers user anxiety measurably; onboarding-friendly;
inclusive and calm; differentiates against corporate blue sameness
without shouting.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** pastel text on pastel ground
(contrast failure); no dark anchor (the page floats away — everything
soft = nothing hierarchical); blob/wave overload; infantilizing an
adult product; pastel + thin grey text (double softness = illegible);
treating pastel as "female design" stereotype rather than tonal choice.

**Combination guidance:** dial saturation up → playful-kids; add cream
canvas + editorial type → warm-editorial storybook (MindMarket sits
between); with healthcare → pediatric/wellness warmth; with SaaS → the
"friendly HR-tech" standard (15five). Pastel washes can soften ONE
section of a sober system, but a half-pastel bank betrays both
grammars.
