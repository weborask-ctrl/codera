# Minimal / Swiss-influenced

**Archetype:** grid, restraint, one family, near-zero ornament — design as
typesetting discipline. Studied exemplars: Cowboy ("porcelain showroom"),
Augen Pro ("Apple keynote on surgical white"), 19–86, AREA 17, Haus Otto,
Mike Matas ("ink on fresh snow").

**Emotional tone:** clarity, order, engineering honesty, quiet authority.
Nothing decorative; everything earns its place.

**Typical audience / business fit:** product companies selling precision
(hardware, mobility, instruments), design-literate B2B, architecture,
consultancies, portfolios. Buyers who equate clarity with competence.

**Poor-fit contexts:** emotional/sensory purchases (food, travel,
children), brands needing warmth or story, content-thin sites (minimalism
exposes emptiness), audiences that need visual guidance and reassurance.

**Canvas / background behavior:** white or near-white; occasional
full-bleed neutral block (grey/black) as structure. No warm tint — the
temperature is deliberately neutral-to-cool. Inversion allowed; tinting
is not.

**Color logic:** black/white/grey ladder; at most one functional accent
used with extreme scarcity (a single blue link color). Product imagery
may carry color; UI does not. Semantic states (error/success) exist but
are visually quiet.

**Typography logic:** one grotesque family (Suisse, Helvetica-class,
Neue Haas, Universal Sans) across all sizes; weights 400–600; hierarchy
via a strict modular scale; generous body lh 1.5; display moderate
(48–96px) — the genre shouts less than editorial. Tabular figures for
any data.

**Spacing / density:** the 8pt grid taken seriously; consistent column
grid (12-col) actually followed; medium density allowed — this is the one
minimal family that can host tables and spec sheets elegantly. Sections
80–120px.

**Layout / composition:** visible grid logic; aligned edges everywhere;
symmetric or systematically asymmetric; captions and metadata in the
margins; whitespace distributed evenly rather than dramatically.

**Imagery direction:** studio photography on white/neutral seamless;
technical drawings; matte product renders. Uniform treatment across all
images — same light, same backdrop family. No lifestyle noise.

**Component language:** rectangles and 2–6px radii; 1px borders; text
buttons and quiet filled buttons in ink; uppercase tracked labels;
underlines as link affordance. Forms are the genre's showpiece — perfect
alignment, clear states.

**Motion behavior:** near-none, and instant — fast fades (150–250ms),
no easing theatrics. State changes snap. Anything springy contradicts
the engineering voice.

**Mobile adaptation:** the grid collapses predictably (12→4 cols);
typography ratio compresses gently; tables become definition lists;
nothing else changes — the genre's predictability is its mobile strength.

**Strengths:** timeless, cheap to maintain, superb for dense factual
content, communicates competence, effortless accessibility (contrast
and structure come free).

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** sterility (no single humane detail);
grey text below AA "for elegance"; grid worship producing boring
sameness; minimalism as an excuse for missing content design; mixing two
grotesques that are 95% identical (pick one).

**Combination guidance:** the neutral chassis under many hybrids: swiss +
warm canvas = premium consumer (Cowboy); swiss + editorial display =
agency voice; swiss + product photography = hardware commerce. Absorbs
one expressive subsystem (a display face OR an accent OR art photography)
— never more than one at a time.
