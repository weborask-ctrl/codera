# Tech / SaaS

**Archetype:** product screenshots, one accent, engineered chrome — the
software-sells-software grammar. Studied exemplars: Attio ("architectural
editorial on white marble"), Linear (dark pole), ElevenLabs (warm pole),
Evernote, Atlassian (enterprise pole), Krepling, Cap, shadcn/ui.

**Emotional tone:** capability, momentum, focus. "This tool will make
you faster" said in the tool's own visual accent.

**Typical audience / business fit:** the default grammar for software:
productivity, devtools, AI products, fintech apps, analytics, security.
Buyers evaluate the product THROUGH the site — the marketing page is
treated as evidence of product quality.

**Poor-fit contexts:** non-software businesses wearing SaaS costume
(restaurants with "Get started" heroes); deeply human services where
product-first framing feels cold; luxury positioning (SaaS grammar reads
mass-market).

**Canvas / background behavior:** three proven poles — quiet white
(Attio: near-white + hairlines), warm paper (ElevenLabs/Evernote), dark
instrument (Linear). Pick ONE pole; the page may borrow a single
opposite-pole band for contrast. Tinted section bands (fog, lavender)
structure long pages.

**Color logic:** ONE chromatic action color owning every button/link/
focus (cobalt #266df0, blue #1868db, lime #94e130); blue-tinted or
brand-tinted shadows if any; decorative accents quarantined to
illustration/tags; semantic states standardized. Weight-500-as-default
text ink (Attio) is a subtle quality signal.

**Typography logic:** Inter-class UI family with Display cut for
marketing (InterDisplay 600 at 40–64px, tracking -0.015–0.02em);
optional serif or whisper-weight moments for editorial warmth
(TiemposText pull-quotes "earned by rarity"); mono for code/metadata
credibility; font-feature-settings (ss03, cv01, zero, tnum) as
craft details.

**Spacing / density:** 80–120px sections; compact density INSIDE cards
and product frames; 1200–1440px max-width; 8px-grid element gaps. The
rhythm: breathe outside, dense inside.

**Layout / composition:** centered hero (headline, subtext, two buttons,
product screenshot card below); logo strip; alternating text/screenshot
splits; 3-col feature cards; pricing table; final CTA band. The genre's
skeleton is standardized — differentiation comes from voice, accent, and
craft, not from reinventing the skeleton.

**Imagery direction:** REAL product screenshots in hairline-border
frames — the screenshot is the proof; abstract product-orb gradients as
atmosphere (ElevenLabs); diagrammatic illustration for architecture;
avatars/logos as social proof; no lifestyle photography unless the
product is human-facing services.

**Component language:** complete, disciplined kit: filled dark or accent
primary + ghost secondary; 6–12px radii (or pills — pick one); inputs
with focus rings; badges/tags in quiet fills; tabs; code blocks styled;
the component craft IS the trust signal for technical buyers.

**Motion behavior:** product-led motion: UI demos animating in frames,
scroll-triggered feature reveals (once per section), hover micro-
feedback <150ms; no scroll-hijacking between the visitor and the
information; skeletons over spinners.

**Mobile adaptation:** screenshots need mobile-specific crops (a desktop
dashboard shrunk to 350px is illegible — crop to the relevant panel);
sticky CTA restraint; tables→cards; performance is credibility
(a slow devtools site is self-refuting).

**Strengths:** conversion-proven skeleton; product does the persuading;
scales content well; component discipline transfers directly into the
product itself.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** template sameness (the skeleton
without a voice); fake screenshots/mockup exaggeration; accent inflation;
gradient-mesh backgrounds from 2021; feature-list walls with no
hierarchy; dark mode chosen for fashion then abandoned mid-page;
screenshot frames inconsistent (mixed radii/shadows).

**Combination guidance:** the most absorbent family — pairs with
warm-editorial (premium calm), dark-cinematic (infra/devtools drama),
corporate (enterprise trust), playful (friendly SaaS), editorial
(thought-leader brands). Rule for every hybrid: the partner style owns
the marketing SHELL; SaaS discipline owns every interactive component.
