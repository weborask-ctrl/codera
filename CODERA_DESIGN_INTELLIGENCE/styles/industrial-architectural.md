# Industrial / Architectural

**Archetype:** blueprint and concrete — hard edges, specimen labels,
material honesty. Studied exemplars: Klim ("typographic gallery in a
black box"), The1 Amsterdam ("building-scale typography on painted
concrete"), 70Materia, Superlative ("industrial product monograph"),
Aspelin Reitan, Factory ("terminal war room").

**Emotional tone:** rigor, permanence, material truth, engineered
competence. The aesthetic of drawings that get built.

**Typical audience / business fit:** architecture practices, construction
and development, manufacturing, industrial products, engineering
consultancies, type foundries, logistics — anywhere "we build real
things" is the message.

**Poor-fit contexts:** warmth-dependent services (care, children,
hospitality unless deliberately austere-luxury), playful consumer apps,
audiences alienated by austerity.

**Canvas / background behavior:** two valid grounds: pale studio
(#f0f0f0, #e5e7eb concrete grey, white) or matte black gallery. Bands
alternate like exhibition rooms; background shifts ARE the dividers
(Klim: "the background color shift IS the divider").

**Color logic:** monochrome + ONE industrial signal color used like
safety marking (Klim's flare orange #d33c03, The1's poster palette on
grey, chartreuse annotations on blueprints). The signal marks labels and
annotations, never large fills. Material colors (rust, concrete, steel
blue) over candy colors.

**Typography logic:** grotesques with technical DNA (Söhne, Suisse,
Teka, GT Pressura, DIN-class); uppercase tracked labels everywhere —
the specimen-label is the genre's atomic unit; display can go
building-scale (100px+) when type replaces imagery; tabular figures,
ordinals, dimension-style metadata (Klim ships tnum/ordn on).

**Spacing / density:** tighter than editorial — 60–80px sections, compact
8–20px element gaps; the genre tolerates functional density (fixture
lists, spec tables) beautifully. Hard grid alignment mandatory.

**Layout / composition:** full-bleed bands stacked like plates in a
monograph; three-column catalogue rows (name / variants / action);
oversized type as architecture; images edge-to-edge, zero radius;
annotation labels floating at plan-drawing positions.

**Imagery direction:** buildings, materials, machines — photographed
frontal and honest, natural light or studio-raw; blueprints, axonometric
drawings, contour lines as decorative system; NO lifestyle softening.
B&W photography is native here.

**Component language:** 0–2px radii ("near-sharp corner is the geometric
signature"); 1px rules; buttons as labeled rectangles or bare tracked
text; tags as stamped labels; tables celebrated, not hidden. Shadows
absent — flat like printed plates.

**Motion behavior:** mechanical and precise — hard cuts, slide-in panels
along grid axes, counters/tickers; no organic easing, no bounce. A
drafting-table aesthetic: things click into place.

**Mobile adaptation:** bands stack cleanly; building-scale type clamps
hard (100px→40px) but keeps uppercase-label structure; spec tables become
stacked label/value rows; keep edges sharp — rounding "for mobile
friendliness" breaks the language.

**Strengths:** massive differentiation from soft SaaS sameness; superb
for portfolios of physical work; type-only sections are cheap and
striking; density-capable.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** brutality without craft (0px radius
+ bad kerning = just harsh); signal color promoted to brand-wide accent;
fake textures (concrete JPEG backgrounds); austerity applied to warm
subjects; illegible tracked-uppercase body text.

**Combination guidance:** with luxury → "architectural luxury" (Aspelin:
candlelit archive) — parchment canvas, industrial structure; with
dark-cinematic → Factory-class terminal authority; with editorial →
design-annual hybrid (Grafik). Absorbs photography-led easily. Keep away
from pastel and playful families entirely.
