# Retro / Craft / Illustrated

**Archetype:** hand-made texture, period voice, personality — design
that admits a human made it. Studied exemplars: pampam.city ("old
explorer's notebook"), MindMarket's paper-cut world, Essie Wine ("dusty
rose chapbook"), General Intelligence Company ("literary journal beside
a bonfire"), jun.works ("printed zine with sticker labels"), 601 Inc.
("warm film marquee"), fal.ai's 8-bit playground.

**Emotional tone:** nostalgia, warmth, wit, authenticity. The
counter-position to algorithmic sameness: "a person with taste chose
this."

**Typical audience / business fit:** indie brands and studios, coffee/
food/craft beverage, publishing and newsletters, boutique agencies,
games and culture products, personal sites, brands selling provenance
and story. Also AI companies deliberately counter-signaling (warm
literary voice against cold-tech expectations).

**Poor-fit contexts:** enterprise trust surfaces, clinical contexts,
luxury-modern positioning, data-dense tools; and any brand faking
heritage it doesn't have — audiences smell costume nostalgia.

**Canvas / background behavior:** aged paper tones (cream, oat,
parchment #faf2ec class), occasionally deep period colors (wine,
forest, marquee black with warm bulbs); texture implied through color
and type, NOT through paper-texture JPEGs (the amateur tell).

**Color logic:** period-coherent palettes (70s mustard/rust/avocado;
50s diner teal/coral; print-era black+one spot color); muted saturation
with 1–2 richer spots; ink is warm near-black; the palette must feel
*printed*, not backlit.

**Typography logic:** the family's engine — period faces used with
scholarship: slab/serif revivals (Nineties, Cahuenga, Reckless,
Cardinal Fruit), mono for typewriter voice, script ONLY as garnish;
pair one characterful display with one quiet workhorse; NEVER two
novelty faces; body stays modern-legible (16px+) — the retro lives at
display and label level.

**Spacing / density:** print-derived rhythm — comfortable margins,
justified-feeling measures, denser than minimalism (zines and journals
have texture); rules, dividers, and ornaments (arrows, asterisks,
stamps) structure the density.

**Layout / composition:** editorial spreads (masthead, columns,
pull-quotes); sticker/label collage moments; ticket/stamp/seal motifs
as components; hand-drawn arrows and annotations; the grid is present
but relaxed — slight rotation/offset as craft signal (±1–2°, not
chaos).

**Imagery direction:** illustration first — woodcut/etching, mid-century
flat, comic linework, pixel-art (retro-digital), risograph grain; film-
grade photography with period grading; product shots styled as catalog
plates; ONE illustration grammar per brand, rigorously.

**Component language:** buttons as stamps/tickets/pills with 1–2px ink
borders; sticker-shadow offsets; tags as printed labels; hover states
like ink spreading or stamp pressing; forms styled as paper forms
(labels above, generous fields).

**Motion behavior:** analog metaphors — flip, stamp, slide-in like a
card dealt; marquee bulbs; typewriter reveals used ONCE; frame-rate
stepping (12fps feel) for retro-digital contexts; motion sparing —
print doesn't move much, and the stillness is part of the voice.

**Mobile adaptation:** print metaphors compress well (a phone is a
pocket zine); keep margins and rules; collage moments simplify to
single stickers; ornament density reduced before type size; tap
targets modernized regardless of period costume.

**Strengths:** instant personality and memorability; strong story
alignment for provenance brands; differentiates hard against SaaS
sameness; illustration systems scale across merch/packaging.

**Failure modes / common mistakes:** texture JPEG wallpaper; novelty-
font body text; pastiche without a specific period (generic "vintage");
kitsch tipping (three ornaments where one lands); illegibility excused
as authenticity; fake heritage claims; mixed illustration eras.

**Combination guidance:** warm-editorial is the natural base (journal
voice); playful-kids for storybook brands (MindMarket); hospitality for
diners/wineries/roasters; brutalist for zine-punk energy (jun.works).
With tech-saas: the "literary AI" counter-signal (General Intelligence
Company) — retro shell, disciplined modern components underneath.
Never with clinical or corporate-procurement surfaces.
